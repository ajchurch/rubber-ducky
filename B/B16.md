# B16. Survey the current state-of-the-art solutions in cybersecurity.

## 1) Agentic SOC (Security Operations Center)

Agentic SOC is an operating model that uses specialised, autonomous AI agents to triage, investigate, and respond to threats. Instead of humans doing manual investigations or following rigid scripts, these AI agents reason, gather evidence, and take action independently, a lot faster than humans manually could.

[CrowdStrike](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-au/solutions/agentic-soc-transformation/) are big players in the Agentic SOC space by shifting from passive AI chatbots to having groups of specialised agents that independently triage alerts, hunt threats, and analyze malware. Powered by their Charlotte AI AgentWorks and Agentic SOAR ecosystem, the platform allows teams to build custom agents and have adaptive responses while maintaining strict bounded autonomy that requires human approval for higher risk containment actions.


## 2) Zero Trust
 
Zero Trust is a framework that replaces traditional networks with a model that requires constant verification instead of a once off 'you've done x so we can trust you'. It continuously authenticates every user and device, limits access to the bare minimum required, and assumes threats are already inside the network to prevent data breaches.

[Palo Alto Networks](https://www.paloaltonetworks.com.au/zero-trust) are one of the main companies in the Zero Trust space. They implement Zero Trust through their Prisma SASE platform, which unifies network security and cloud delivered security into a single system. Their technology continuously verifies the identity and security posture of every user and device, secures hybrid workforces, and uses AI to block threats in real time before they can spread.

## 3) Post-Quantum Cryptography Migration

Due to the rise of quantum computing, a lot of our current cryptographic methods are at risk of getting cracked. It is the urgent, long-term technology initiative to replace vulnerable public-key encryption algorithms (like RSA) with new quantum-resistant algorithms.

The current issue is that cyber-adversaries are actively stealing and archiving encrypted, sensitive data today to decrypt it later (something called 'Harvest Now, Decrypt Later' (HNDL)).

[IBM](https://www.ibm.com/think/topics/quantum-safe-cryptography) has established itself as a pioneer in both the creation of quantum computers and the simultaneous development of the cryptography required to defeat them. Being on both the offensive and defensive sides of the equation.

On the offensive side, they launching the first cloud-accessible quantum system, scaling processors past the 1000 qubit barrier, and building the industry standard software ecosystem, [Qiskit](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/qiskit). Defensively, IBM researchers co-developed three of the primary post-quantum cryptography algorithms officially standardised by NIST to replace vulnerable encryption. By embedding these quantum safe protections directly into their commercial mainframes and software suites, IBM has positioned itself as both the creator of the quantum future and its primary security architect.

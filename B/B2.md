# B2. Discover 5 unique strong security implementations.

## 1) Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
Requiring something you know (password) plus something you have (phone, hardware key) or something you are (fingerprint). Even if an attacker steals your password, they still can't log in. App-based codes (Google Authenticator, Authy), email OTP, SMS codes, and hardware keys are common forms of MFA people use today to have an extra form of security for their accounts.

## 2) HTTPS / TLS Encryption
The padlock in your browser. TLS encrypts traffic between your browser and a website, preventing eavesdropping and tampering on the network. It also verifies you're actually talking to the real site via certificates issued by trusted authorities it is pretty much what every legitimate website uses now, and browsers warn users when it's missing.

## 3) Password Hashing with Salting
Instead of storing passwords as plain text, websites store a one-way hash (using algorithms like bcrypt) combined with a unique random 'salt' per user. Even if the database leaks, attackers can't easily reverse the hashes to get original passwords, and the salt prevents them from using precomputed rainbow tables.

## 4) Firewalls
Firewalls control what network traffic is allowed in or out based on rules. Modern next-gen firewalls go beyond simple port blocking to inspect application layer traffic, detect intrusions, and block known malicious sources. Most home routers have a basic one built in.

## 5) Email Spam and Phishing Filters
Services like Gmail, and Outlook scan incoming email for malicious links, suspicious attachments, spoofed senders, and known phishing patterns. Combined with protocols like SPF, DKIM, and DMARC that verify sender authenticity, they block the vast majority of phishing attempts before users ever see them.
